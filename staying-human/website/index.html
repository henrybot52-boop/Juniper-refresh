Where we stand

Not anti-AI. Pro-human.

We use AI every day — including to build this company. The line we draw isn't against the tools. It's around the things that only mean something when a person does them.

Gladly handed to the machines

  • Finding the lost recipe
  • Translating grandmother's letters
  • Transcribing oral histories before they fade
  • The planning, the scheduling, the inviting

Kept in human hands

  • The kneading
  • The telling — out loud, badly, lovingly
  • The harmony parts nobody wrote down
  • The showing up

Some things should stay inefficient. The point of baking bread with your kid is not the bread.

The living archive

Eight things worth doing ourselves

Food & Cooking
Recipes with lineage, cooked by hand. Not just ingredients — who taught it, and to whom.
Storytelling
Oral history and family lore, recorded so it keeps getting told — in rooms, to faces.
Music & Song
Singing together, learned by ear. Lullabies, rounds, and the parts you only catch standing next to someone.
Crafts & Making
Things shaped by human hands — and the tricks you only learn by watching someone else's.
Rituals & Celebrations
Holidays, rites of passage, seasons. The choreography of meaning, kept danceable.
Games & Play
Play that needs people in a room — house rules, backyard rules, the game only your family knows.
Letters & Correspondence
Words written slowly, on purpose. Mail that arrives late and is kept for decades.
Gatherings & Community
The table, the fire, the front porch — the container that holds everything else.

How it works

Off the page, into rooms

Workshops

A tradition keeper teaches a practice hands-on — bread, letterpress, harmony singing, whittling. Small groups, real materials, phones in a basket by the door.

Seasonal gatherings

Events tied to the calendar — harvest tables, solstice fires, new-year letter-writing nights. Open to all. AI plans the logistics; people bring the rest.

Tradition Keepers

Elders and practitioners matched with learners for sustained, person-to-person transmission — apprenticeship for the ordinary sacred things.
