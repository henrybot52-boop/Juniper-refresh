Staying Human preserves, practices, and passes on the traditions that make us who we are — cooking together, telling stories, singing, making things by hand, writing letters, gathering around tables and fires.

Not against technology. Just for humans.

Why now

When everything can be generated,
the ungenerated becomes precious.

AI can write, paint, compose, and converse. Soon it may do most of the work. That changes what humans have to do — but not what makes a life feel like ours.

Traditions are how people have always encoded meaning: in bread, in song, in stories told badly and lovingly, in the way a holiday smells. Every tradition skips one generation and it's gone. The work is now.

“Some things should be inefficient.
The point of baking bread with your kid is not the bread.” — The Staying Human Manifesto

The living archive

Eight pillars of staying human

🍞

Food & Cooking

Recipes with lineage, cooked by hand. Not just ingredients — who taught it, and to whom.

🔥

Storytelling

Oral history and family lore, recorded so it keeps getting told — out loud, in rooms.

🎶

Music & Song

Singing together, learned by ear. Lullabies, rounds, and the harmony parts nobody wrote down.

🧶

Crafts & Making

Things shaped by human hands — and the tricks you only learn by watching someone else's.

🕯️

Rituals & Celebrations

Holidays, rites of passage, seasons. The choreography of meaning, kept danceable.

🎲

Games & Play

Play that needs people in a room — house rules, backyard games, the game only your family knows.

✉️

Letters & Correspondence

Words written slowly, on purpose. Mail that arrives late and is kept for decades.

🪑

Gatherings & Community

The table, the fire, the front porch — the container that holds all the other pillars.

Off the page, into rooms

How we do it

01

Workshops

Hands-on sessions where a tradition keeper teaches a practice — bread, letterpress, harmony singing, whittling. Small groups, no screens, real materials.

02

Seasonal Gatherings

Quarterly events tied to the calendar — harvest tables, solstice fires, new-year letter-writing nights. Open to all.

03

Tradition Keepers

Elders and practitioners matched with learners for sustained, person-to-person transmission — apprenticeship for the ordinary sacred things.
